# Win Sri Lanka through compliance and verticals

BizzFlow should not sell itself as "all-in-one ERP" to everyone. It should launch as the **VAT-compliance-ready, locally priced POS + accounting + statutory payroll platform**, and lead with a few beachhead verticals: automotive workshops and spare-parts dealers, bought-leaf tea factories, and small hotels and restaurants. The other modules are upsells, not headline features. Three facts support this. First, the realistic paid market is small: roughly **81,500 small and medium firms** at the last census, plus a slice of micro retailers and restaurants. Second, local cloud POS already prices at **LKR 3,000–10,000/month**, led by POS Masters and its separate vertical suites. Third, 2026–27 brings forced change. A **revised mandatory VAT invoice format takes effect 1 October 2026**. **IRD e-invoicing over a RAMIS API** is being phased in. The VAT Amendment Act No. 14 of 2026 legally requires **CGIR-approved "secured POS machines"**, though no start date is set. Core **PDPA obligations start 1 January 2027**. No competitor in the notes combines POS, ERP, statutory HR/payroll and several vertical modules in one LKR-priced subscription. That gap is real, but it is narrow and it will close. A sensible illustrative target is a few thousand paying customers over 3 years, reached through compliance-led messaging, hardware-dealer and accountant resellers, and annual prepaid plans priced near the LKR 80,000/year market anchor. Market-size and willingness-to-pay data are weak, so treat every number here as a planning range to test, not a forecast.

## About 80,000 firms form the paying core

Sri Lanka has about **1.02 million establishments**, but over 90% are sole proprietorships and about 58% are unregistered. Only **71,126 small and 10,405 medium** establishments were counted in the 2013/14 Economic Census, which is still the latest full census ([LIRNEasia/DCS](https://lirneasia.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/LIRNEaisa-ICT-access-and-use-by-SMEs-in-Sri-Lanka.pdf)). The base is also churning. More than **200,000 MSMEs closed between 2018 and 2022** ([Ada Derana](https://www.adaderana.lk/news/120977/over-200000-smes-ceased-operations-between-20182022-says-deputy-minister)). A sharper proxy for the businesses that matter most to BizzFlow is the **36,656 VAT-registered taxpayers** as of June 2026 ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/top-story/IRD-facing-roadblocks-in-digital-POS-rollout/26-796303)). These are the firms the invoice, e-invoicing and secured-POS rules hit first, and they can afford software.

An illustrative bottom-up estimate puts theoretical TAM at roughly **LKR 3.6–30bn a year (about US$12–100M)**. That is 100–250k reachable firms paying LKR 3,000–10,000 a month. This is an inference, not a sourced figure, and no Sri Lanka-specific ERP/POS market report exists. Globally, POS software is about **US$16bn, growing ~12% a year**, and SMEs hold ~60% of it ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/pos-software-market)).

BizzFlow's verticals vary in how well they can be measured. Hospitality is the best counted: registered tourism establishments rose from **4,390 to 4,927 in 2025** (+12%) ([Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/print/business-news/Sri-Lankas-tourism-investments-hit-six-year-high-in-2025-amid-substantial-capacity-expansion/273-338793)). Arrivals hit a record **2.36M**, but daily spend was revised down from $171 to $148 ([SLTDA](https://www.sltda.gov.lk/storage/common_media/Year_in_Review_2025_Final_updated_Report_2026_04_02.pdf)). Lower spend per guest pushes operators toward cost control. Automotive demand is structural. The fleet is about **8.82M vehicles**, and a record **311,592 registrations** came in the first 11 months of 2025 after the import ban was lifted ([Ada Derana/DMT](https://www.adaderana.lk/news/115789/223000-motorcycles-55000-cars-and-13000-three-wheelers-registered-in-2025-dmt); [Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/breaking-news/Motorcycles-lead-vehicle-registrations-in-2025-DMT-reports/108-327355)). That fleet will need servicing and parts for years. Tea output was about **264M kg in 2025** ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Tea-output-edges-up-in-2025-despite-weather-shocks-fertiliser-constraints/44-786846)), but there is no verified count of factories. Nobody has a current count of garages, shops or restaurants.

The economy now supports buying software, but cautiously. GDP grew **5.0% in 2025**, core inflation was 2.7%, the policy rate fell to 7.75% and private credit rose 25% ([CBSL](https://www.cbsl.gov.lk/sites/default/files/cbslweb_documents/press/pr/press_20260420_aer_2025_e.pdf); [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/country/srilanka/fragments/strategy)). Digital readiness is uneven. Internet use reached **13.9M people by end-2025**, yet 40% of the population is still offline ([Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/front-page/40-3-per-cent-of-Sri-Lankans-still-offline-in-2025/238-331925)), and only **21.3% of households own a computer** ([AsiaNews](https://www.asianews.it/en/south-asia/sri-lanka/sri-lanka-s-digital-winter-only-213-per-cent-of-households-own-a-computer)). That makes BizzFlow's Flutter mobile POS and offline-tolerant desktop POS core features, not extras. Cost and a lack of skilled staff are the top SME barriers to digitising ([Peradeniya PGUM](https://pgum.pdn.ac.lk/?p=5508)).

## Competitors are siloed, so integration is the wedge

The market splits into three tiers, and none of them overlaps fully with BizzFlow.

The first tier is local vertical POS SaaS. **POS Masters** is the closest analogue. It sells ten separate products (retail, CloudChef restaurant, Auto+ garage, bar, laundry, repair, pawning, distribution) at a typical **Rs 7,500/month or Rs 80,000/year** ([POS Masters](https://posmasters.lk/); [Auto+](https://posmasters.lk/products/auto-plus)). Auto+ already covers job cards, parts and lubricant stock, mechanics, debtors and per-chassis vehicle history, so BizzFlow's garage module has a direct rival. POS Masters' customer claims contradict each other (1,395 businesses on one page, 13 live RetailPos clients and 51 mapped installs elsewhere). The incumbent is probably smaller than it markets itself. Below it sit one-time-licence products such as **Ceylond from LKR 79,900** ([Ceylond](https://ceylond-pos.pages.dev/)), hardware-bundled cloud POS (Option4), Fiverr freelancers and free open-source POS ([PCWorld.lk](https://pcworld.lk/magazine/?p=146)).

The second tier is mid-market ERP: **Bileeta Entution** (sold through HNB and SLT), Blue Lotus 360, SAP Business ByDesign partners, IFS and Infor. **Four of the top ten tea exporters run IFS**, and Basilur runs Infor ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.lk/ifs-delivering-cutting-edge-business-solutions/?print=pdf); [FT](https://www.ft.lk/it-telecom-tech/Kingslake-streamlines-Basilur-Tea-operations-with-Infor-CSI/50-707516)). These vendors don't publish prices and need heavy implementation work.

The third tier is international SaaS reaching the market through partners. **Zoho reported 50%+ YoY revenue growth in Sri Lanka** and resells through SLT-Mobitel, Dialog and iDeal Tech ([FT.lk](https://www.ft.lk/it-telecom-tech/Zoho-positions-for-business-growth-in-Sri-Lanka-promoting-digital-transformation/50-772293); [Daily Mirror](https://dailymirror.lk/business-news/SLT-Mobitel-with-EGUARDIAN-to-enhance-Zoho-accessibility-for-MSMEs-in-Sri-Lanka/273-297142)). Odoo bills in INR and needs a partner ([oec.sh](https://oec.sh/odoo-pricing/sri-lanka)). Payroll is a separate category (MiHCM, Gallery HR) ([MiHCM](https://mihcm.com/en-lk/solutions/payroll/)).

| Segment | Main rivals | BizzFlow angle |
|---|---|---|
| Garage / workshop | POS Masters Auto+ (Rs 7,500/mo), custom builds | Job cards **plus** parts accounting, payroll, fleet; existing auto references |
| Restaurant / KOT | CloudChef, OMAK, Leapset | KOT + thermal bridge + inventory + accounting in one bill |
| Hotel / banquet | Boost Hotels, myhotelina, IDS Next, Syntax | Front office + banquet + restaurant for small hotels; **no banquet-specific vendor found** |
| Tea manufacturing | IFS, Infor, SAP (large); Agrithmics (field data) | Production/BOM + bought-leaf supplier ledgers for **small factories, apparently underserved** |
| Retail | POS Masters, Option4, one-time licences, Zoho | Compliance-ready invoicing + LankaQR |
| Payroll | MiHCM, Gallery HR | Bundled EPF/ETF/APIT, not a separate subscription |

Independent reviews of local vendors are almost nonexistent, so the claim that competitors are weak on integration rests on how their products are packaged. It does not come from customer complaints. Steelman of the opposing view: SMEs may prefer simple single-purpose tools, and "all-in-one" can read as complex and expensive. That argues for selling one module first and unlocking more later, not for selling the full suite on day one.

## Price annual plans around the LKR 80,000 anchor

Buyers already have three reference prices. Cloud POS costs **LKR 3,000–5,000/month basic and 6,000–10,000 advanced** ([Merpacc](https://merpacc.com/blog/pos-system-price-sri-lanka.html)). A one-time licence costs about LKR 80,000. Indian billing apps are extremely cheap: **Vyapar at INR 1,849/year**, myBillBook at INR 3,490–3,990/year ([Accountune](https://www.accountune.com/vyapar-vs-accountune-vs-mybillbook)). BizzFlow's annual plans should land at about **LKR 40–80k/year for a single outlet**, with a 10–15% prepay discount. That is in line with POS Masters, which charges Rs 80k/year against Rs 90k on monthly billing. Multi-module bundles should run **LKR 10–15k/month**, with add-ons for payroll per employee, hotel or banquet, and extra branches. Frame the annual plan against the one-time licence: "updates, cloud backup, compliance changes and support included." The upcoming invoice-format and e-invoicing changes make that argument concrete, because a frozen licence will not adapt to them.

For the trial, global benchmarks show **card-required trials convert ~30%+ versus ~3–8% without a card** ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/saas-statistics)). Card penetration in Sri Lanka is low, though, so BizzFlow's no-card 14-day trial is right. It needs human onboarding over WhatsApp in Sinhala and Tamil to lift conversion. Khatabook's hardest problem was persuading and training offline merchants to switch, and language support was central to its growth ([CIO & Leader](https://www.cioandleader.com/?p=3363)). Use three billing rails: **PayHere recurring cards** (no extra subscription fee) ([PayHere](https://support.payhere.lk/api-&-mobile-sdk/recurring-api)), annual bank transfer with manual activation, and LankaQR one-off payments. Annual prepayment also protects against SMB churn, which benchmarks put at 3–5% of customers a month ([smbs.com](https://www.smbs.com/statistics/saas-churn-rate)).

Hardware is a lever. DirectPay rents a Sunmi terminal for **LKR 3,500/month** and waives the fee above a processing volume ([DirectPay](https://dp-web.directpay.lk/pos/)). A BizzFlow bundle of an Android terminal, thermal printer (driven through the print bridge) and annual plan, financed or rented through a dealer, removes the upfront hurdle.

## Regulation in 2026–27 creates a demand window

Regulation is BizzFlow's strongest go-to-market argument.

The **revised tax-invoice format applies to all VAT-registered persons from 1 October 2026** (Gazette 2500/106). It requires 9-digit supplier and purchaser TINs, continuous invoice numbers in the YYMMM_QQQQ_XXXXX structure, "date of supply", LKR to 2 decimals, and the payment method ([Comarch](https://comarch.com/trade-and-services/data-management/legal-regulation-changes/sri-lanka-postpones-revised-vat-invoice-format-amid-wider-vat-reforms)). Daily FT asks openly whether SMEs are ready ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/columns/VAT-Invoice-Reform-2026-A-necessary-step-forward-but-are-SMEs-ready/4-792457)).

**IRD e-invoicing** began with voluntary Phase I in October 2025 (340+ companies, including exporters and tea firms). Phase II started around June 2026, reaching 170 manufacturers. ERPs connect through a **RAMIS web API** ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/IRD-expands-digital-invoicing-rollout-as-second-phase-gets-underway/44-793040)).

New **s.64B** requires registered persons to use **CGIR-approved secured POS machines** with real-time data capture, within 3 months of a date still to be set ([De Saram](https://www.desaram.com/sri-lanka-vat-changes-2026-key-updates-businesses-must-know/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-sri-lanka-amendments-to-vat-act.html)). The timing is uncertain. One report expects the POS phase by end-2026, while another describes roadblocks and a horizon of about two years ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/top-story/IRD-facing-roadblocks-in-digital-POS-rollout/26-796303)). Plan for both, and don't stake the business on a fixed date.

The same Act puts **18% VAT on non-resident digital services** from July 2026 for B2C supplies, which narrows Zoho's and Odoo's price advantage with non-VAT-registered buyers. Whether the VAT threshold stays at LKR 60m or falls to LKR 36m is disputed. Check this on ird.gov.lk, because a lower threshold would sharply expand the population that must comply.

Payroll must handle **EPF 8%/12%, ETF 3%** and the 2025/26 **APIT tables**, with a tax-free amount of **LKR 150k/month** ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/lk/pdf/kpmg-tax-news/2025/march-2025/Tax-Flash-News-APIT-Tables.pdf); [Ramco](https://www.ramco.com/payce/payroll-compliance-sri-lanka)). Store these as tables that can be updated each year of assessment. Sources disagree on whether a 12% APIT band remains.

The **PDPA's core controller and processor duties begin 1 January 2027** (Gazette 2498/16) ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Data-protection-compliance-regime-takes-effect-on-1-Jan-2027/44-795776)). BizzFlow needs a data processing agreement template, a designated DPO, breach procedures, export and erasure tools, and a review of offshore hosting. Done early, this becomes a selling point to hotels and HR buyers.

Two smaller items follow. E-commerce storefronts should ship with the **CAA 2023 disclosure** fields: seller identity, refunds, warranty and privacy ([Gazette 2332/15](https://www.documents.gov.lk/view/extra-gazettes/2023/5/2332-15_E.pdf)). Payments should go through licensed gateways, not a proprietary wallet, to stay outside CBSL's MPAP circular ([CBSL](https://www.cbsl.gov.lk/sites/default/files/cbslweb_documents/laws/cdg/psd_circular_no_01_of_2026_e.pdf)). Separately, the **zero MDR on LankaQR payments up to Rs 5,000** from April 2026, with about 450,000 accepting merchants, makes an integrated LankaQR display a concrete POS feature ([FT.lk](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Govt-waives-QR-payment-fees-for-small-merchants-in-push-towards-cash-lite-economy/44-790530); [NewsFirst](https://english.newsfirst.lk/2026/04/06/sri-lanka-pushes-cash%E2%80%91lite-economy-with-lankaqr-launch)).

## Recommended go-to-market sequence

**Product, next 0–3 months.** Ship the October 2026 invoice template, with TIN validation and the mandated numbering, and VAT schedule exports. Register interest with the IRD for the Phase II e-invoicing API and build the RAMIS connector. Watch for the s.64B secured-POS specification and aim to be among the first approved vendors. That approval would be a lasting advantage, since an approved list limits who can compete. Polish Sinhala and Tamil UI and receipts, offline POS mode, LankaQR, and WhatsApp receipts.

**Beachheads, 0–12 months.** Start where BizzFlow has references and competitors are thin. In automotive, combine workshop and parts dealer modules with payroll, using existing clients as case studies to take on Auto+ on breadth. In tea, target small and mid bought-leaf factories, not the exporters locked into IFS and SAP. The pitch is production/BOM, supplier leaf ledgers and advances, payroll, and e-invoicing readiness, which matters because tea firms were early e-invoicing participants. In hospitality, sell to small hotels a bundle of front office, banquet and restaurant KOT, where no banquet-specific vendor was found. Keep generic retail as an inbound, self-serve channel and don't chase it.

**Channels.** Run WhatsApp and Facebook demand generation into live demos, but scale through partners, following the regional pattern. Vyapar claims **27,000+ partners**, though the figure is unverified ([Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/1-cr-businesses-27000-partners-and-growing-how-vyapar-is-powering-indias-msme-revolution)). Three partner types fit. POS hardware dealers can bundle terminals with plans. Accounting and tax firms, which are under invoice-reform pressure, can earn recurring commission. Sector bodies can help in the verticals, such as auto parts associations and tea smallholder and factory groups. In year 2, pursue a bank or telco distribution deal, the route Bileeta took with HNB and SLT ([Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/print/financial-news/HNB-partners-with-Bileeta-to-offer-tech-support-to-SMEs/265-240012)) and Zoho with SLT-Mobitel and Dialog. Pitch it as a compliance-ready bundle for their SME customers. Also align with the government programmes that target doubling SME digital adoption by 2030, such as the FITIS MSME tool platform proposal ([MEA TechWatch](https://meatechwatch.com/2025/08/05/fitis-unveils-roadmap-to-achieve-15-billion-digital-economy-in-sri-lanka-by-2030/)).

**Commercial model.** Keep the 14-day no-card trial, with a guided WhatsApp onboarding call in the first 48 hours. Make annual plans the default. Charge a modest paid setup or data migration fee, which can be waived for annual prepay. Offer a starter POS tier priced against one-time licences, and grow revenue through module and branch add-ons. Track trial-to-paid rate, time to first invoice, and annual renewal as the core metrics. No Sri Lankan benchmarks exist, so BizzFlow's own cohorts will be the first real data.

## Conclusion

BizzFlow's breadth is an advantage only if it reaches buyers in a form they can understand. In Sri Lanka in 2026–27, that form is compliance. The invoice format, e-invoicing, the secured-POS mandate and the PDPA turn software from optional into necessary for the roughly 37,000 VAT-registered firms and the larger group behind them. A local vendor that already handles EPF/ETF/APIT, LankaQR, thermal printing and vertical workflows is far better placed to meet those rules than siloed POS apps or USD-priced foreign suites. Timing is the main risk: the secured-POS mandate could slip by two years. So the mandate should be the reason buyers act now, while BizzFlow builds a business that does not depend on its date.

The biggest unknowns are how many garages, factories and shops actually exist, what SMEs will pay, and whether Sri Lankan buyers want one integrated system or several simple apps. Answer them cheaply before scaling: run 60–90 day pilots in each beachhead vertical, test two price points, and do a short survey through partner accountants. Then decide which verticals to put sales resources behind.
